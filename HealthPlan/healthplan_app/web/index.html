Step 1

What we compare

Every plan gets the same five numbers, and the lowest yearly total wins.

  1. 01PremiumWhat you pay for the plan all year, used or not. It never depends on your health (HealthCare.gov).
  2. 02Care costsWhat your visits, medication or a birth cost before the plan pays anything.
  3. 03DeductibleYou pay all your care up to this amount, then a set share of each bill.
  4. 04Out-of-pocket limitThe most you pay for care in a year. Past it, the plan pays everything.
  5. 05Yearly totalPremium plus what you pay for care. This is the number we rank.

Where the numbers come from public sources
  • Premium share: Silver uses what workers pay on average in 2025, $1,440 a year for single coverage and $6,850 for family. Bronze is half of that, Gold 1.6×. KFF Employer Health Benefits Survey 2025
  • Deductible and coinsurance: Silver uses the average single deductible, $1,886, and the average 20% coinsurance for a hospital stay. same KFF survey
  • Out-of-pocket limit: the 2027 legal maximum is $12,000 for one person and $24,000 for a family. Bronze sits at that cap. CMS, 2027 parameters guidance
  • Having a baby: pregnancy, birth and postpartum care average $20,416 in employer plans, and a newborn's first months $5,820. Peterson-KFF, Sept 2025
  • Deadline: Dec 15 is the last day to pick a plan on HealthCare.gov for coverage that starts Jan 1. Employer deadlines vary. HealthCare.gov
  • Light, some and ongoing care ($300, $1,500, $6,000 a year) are example prices, not from a source. Change them with a key.

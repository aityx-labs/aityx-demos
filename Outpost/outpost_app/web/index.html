Keep the city's lights on.

You run the power of a growing city. Get ready by day, write the rules for the night, and see if the city makes it to morning. Every night the city grows and the night gets harder. How many can it survive?

The rules of the city

The numbers aityx uses.

Each house uses 2 energy per hour of night, streetlights 3, the hospital 4, the factory 6. At dusk the battery holds what your panels made by day (panels × sunlight × 4), up to its capacity. Turbines add wind every hour.

Every house-hour in the dark costs 3 points of approval, every hour of dark streetlights costs 1. You pass a night if the hospital never goes dark and approval stays at 50% or more. 90% and up is three stars.

From night 6 the city keeps growing: a market, a school, a pharmacy, a fire station and a library open, one per night. Each uses as much energy as a house and counts like one. After night 10 every night has a little less sun. There is no last night: the game is how many nights your city survives.

aityx runs all of this as a readable model: same city, same rules, same night, every time. This page draws the result. It calculates nothing.

On the map, the glowing lines on the ground show the idea of the grid: sun and wind feed the battery yard, a main line runs along the main road, and branches reach the houses, the hospital, the factory and the streetlights. Their colours follow what aityx says happened each hour. The model counts energy for the whole city; it does not route individual wires.
